SLIDE 1 (title)
Operators are symbols that perform actions on values. In this lesson you will learn arithmetic, comparison, logical, and assignment operators.

SLIDE 2 (arithmetic operators)
The basic arithmetic operators are plus, minus, multiply, divide, and modulus. Modulus gives the remainder after division. Watch out: when both numbers are int, the division drops the decimal part. Use a double, such as 10.0 divided by 3, to keep decimals.

SLIDE 3 (comparison operators)
Comparison operators return true or false. Equal to uses two equal signs. Not equal to, greater than, less than, greater or equal, and less or equal work the same way.

SLIDE 4 (logical operators)
Logical operators combine boolean values. AND uses two ampersands. OR uses two vertical bars. NOT uses an exclamation mark.

SLIDE 5 (assignment and increment)
The single equals sign assigns a value. Shortcuts such as plus-equals add to a variable. The increment operator, written as two plus signs, adds one.

SLIDE 6 (common mistakes)
Three common mistakes. First, using one equals sign where you mean two. Second, expecting decimals from int division. Third, forgetting that modulus gives the remainder, not the quotient.

SLIDE 7 (recap)
You learned arithmetic, comparison, logical, and assignment operators. Remember that modulus gives the remainder, and that two equal signs compare. Tomorrow we take input from the user using Scanner.
